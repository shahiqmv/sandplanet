The idea in one paragraph

Every form you used to keep in Excel now lives in one place. You fill it in, Planet numbers it, files it in the right register, and produces the PDF you send to the client. The registers maintain themselves.

But the real point is underneath: every material bought, every payment made, every hour worked flows into one number per project — what it has cost so far, against what you are being paid. Nothing gets lost, nothing gets typed twice, and at any moment anyone with the right to know can see exactly where a project stands.

Signing in & your dashboard

Your account decides what you see. Sign in and you land straight on your own dashboard — no menus to hunt, no site to choose. Site staff see only their island. Head Office sees all of them. Your Project Manager sees every site they run. Accounts are created by the Administrator; there is no self-registration, and passwords are stored securely on the server.

On your phone. Planet works in a phone browser and can be added to your home screen. Take DPR photos straight from the camera; drafts are held safely if the signal drops and sync when you're back online — don't retype anything.

Your dashboard answers one question: what needs me today?

Your obligations sit at the top. Below them, the things waiting on you — most urgent first, each saying what's wrong, how old it is, and the one button that deals with it. Below that, the numbers, each with its meaning attached: not "3 MRs" but "3 MRs open — oldest 6 days."

The same idea, shaped for each role

How every document works

There are thirteen document types. They all behave the same way, so learn it once.

  1. Create. Pick the type, fill it in. Your site is already filled in and locked — you cannot file paperwork under another island. The reference (DPR-SJR-001) is issued by Planet, in order, with no gaps.
  2. Save as draft as often as you like. Nothing is final until you issue it.
  3. Submit or issue. Some documents need approval first. Once issued, the document locks — it is the official record.
  4. The PDF generates automatically, on company letterhead, matching your old Excel layout. That is what goes to the client or vendor.
  5. The register updates itself. You never maintain a register again.
Made a mistake after issuing? You don't edit — you revise. MARs and MRs keep their number and step a revision (R0 → R1), with changed lines flagged. A rejected IR returns as a new IR quoting the old one. Anything else can be voided with a reason, and the register keeps the record. Approval stamps print the approver's name, role and exact time on the PDF — that is the digital signature.

The site documents

DPR — Daily Progress Report

Weather, rain and time lost, work done with progress percentages, manpower by trade, machinery, key material stock, matters affecting progress, visitors, safety. At least four captioned photos, or Planet won't let you issue it. Goes to the client every working day — issue it without waiting for your PM; the PM verifies afterwards.

TWS — Tomorrow Work Schedule

Goes out with the DPR before the day ends: planned activities, planned manpower, and what you need from the client tomorrow — buggy, villa access, power isolation.

DMA — Daily Manpower Allocation · internal

Each morning the site turns yesterday's TWS into a crew plan: who works where, on which task, under which project. The Site Engineer drafts it and the PM issues it. It is an internal control document — it is not sent to the client — and it feeds the "Manpower today" figures on the dashboard, flagging idle crew when attendance outruns allocation.

IR — Inspection Request

Submit at least 24 hours ahead. Your PM approves before it goes to the client. Record the client's result — approved, approved with comments, or rejected. Comments close out in Part C with evidence; rejected work returns as a new IR.

MAR — Material Approval Request

Getting a material approved before ordering: description, manufacturer, origin, specification and drawing references, attachments. The PM approves, then it goes to the client. Never order material before written approval.

MR — Material Requisition

The only way a site asks for materials. One MR per loading, all trades. Pick items from the catalogue; enter what you need and what's physically on site, and Planet calculates what to order. Reach Head Office at least seven working days before the boat. PM approval is mandatory. A new requirement before loading? Amend the same MR — don't raise a second one.

GRN — Goods Received Note

When the boat arrives, choose the manifest and every item loads automatically with the shipped quantities. Count physically, enter what actually arrived, and Planet calculates shortages and flags them red. Report shortages or damage within 24 hours — Planet starts that clock and notifies Head Office.

PYR — Payment Request

For spending that isn't materials: boat hire, subcontractors, equipment rental, permits, repairs. Attach the bill or quotation; if there isn't one, say so and give the reason — above a set amount an attachment or a PM override is required. Then it travels: you → your PM → HO Director → a company signatory → Finance pays.

Head Office documents

PR — Procurement Requisition

Raised against MRs: vendors, quotations, terms, amounts. Every local purchase is either cash or credit — never part-paid. On authorisation the purchase order issues on company letterhead.

LM — Loading Manifest

What's on the boat: vessel, destination, quantities loaded, quantities still pending. Pending items go automatically into the Pending Items Log. The destination site raises its GRN straight from this manifest.

The item catalogue & registers

One company-wide list of materials, each with a fixed description and unit. Build an MR by searching the catalogue and picking items — no more "steel pipe" on one form and "25mm SS316 pipe, 6m" on another. Need something not listed? Type it as free text; it's flagged as a new item and you're never blocked. Head Office reviews it and either matches it to something existing or adds it properly. Because everyone uses the same item, one product can be followed all the way through: requested, purchased, loaded, received.

Registers

Every document type has a register, and every register builds itself. Filter by date or status; export any register to Excel or PDF. The DPR & TWS register lists every working day, so a day with no report shows as a visible gap — Fridays are off by default and never flagged. Head Office holds master registers across all sites, plus the Pending Items Log: every item that missed a boat, why, and which boat it's going on next. It clears itself when the item ships.

Petty cash

Each site holds a fixed float with a named custodian — the Site Admin.

Recording spending. Date, amount, what it was for, which cost head, and a photo of the receipt. No receipt? Say so and give the reason. Anything above the per-transaction cap is blocked — that spending goes through a PYR instead. The PM approves the entries in a batch, weekly or at replenishment.

Getting the money back. When the balance runs low, raise a replenishment request. Planet fills in the amount and attaches the whole expense schedule with receipts — you type nothing. It travels the normal PYR route, and when Finance pays it the float restores itself and the cycle closes. Counting. Once a month, count the cash and enter what you counted; Planet compares it to what it thinks you should have and records any difference with your explanation.

Timesheets and payroll

Employees (HR / Payroll). Name, passport, nationality, job category, basic pay, work permit and expiry. Basic pay and passport numbers are visible only to HR and the Administrator — never to site staff.

Daily attendance (Site Admin). One screen, the whole crew, times pre-filled from the site's working hours. Change the exceptions, mark absences and leave, enter overtime.

Overtime approval (PM). Overtime counts only once the PM approves it; unapproved hours are visible but can never reach payroll. Month close. The PM signs off and the site's timesheet locks. When every site is locked, HR exports the payroll figures. A locked month reopens only through HR, with a reason, recorded.

Money: how a payment actually travels

Worth understanding, because it explains why things wait where they wait.

  1. You raise a PR or PYR.
  2. Your PM approves it (PYR only).
  3. The Director approves it — this is commercial approval: should we buy this?
  4. A signatory — one of the two executive directors — authorises it. This is the moment the money is committed to the project. Either director can do it. They may also send it back for review, with a reason, and nothing is committed.
  5. Finance pays it and records the transfer or cheque reference. Finance does not authorise; Finance disburses.
The person approving spending is never the person releasing it. A credit purchase is authorised now and paid later on terms — it appears in Finance's payables until settled.

Projects and cost

A site in Planet is a project: created when the contract is awarded, closed when the work is done. It holds scope, contract value, dates, client and consultant contacts, and its assigned PM — and that assignment routes every approval on that site. Projects run Awarded → Active → On Hold → Closed. Awarded lets you buy before the site opens; Closed stops new documents but keeps everything readable forever.

Cost tracking — three numbers, three meanings

Every purchase, payment, petty-cash entry and month of staff cost flows into the project automatically. You see three numbers, and they mean different things:

  • Committed — authorised and binding, not yet paid.
  • Incurred — what the project has actually consumed. Materials count when they land on site, not when they're ordered.
  • Paid — cash out of the company.

Each cost carries a cost head — Materials, Labour & Staff, Subcontract, Plant & Equipment, Transport & Freight, Site Overheads, Permits & Fees, Other — so you can see where the money went, and click any figure to reach the document behind it. The Projects page shows percentage of contract consumed beside percentage of duration elapsed; if the first runs ahead of the second, that project needs attention.

Cost figures are confidential: Head Office, the Administrator, Senior Management, and the project's own PM. Site users see no cost data — only their own petty-cash balance and the payment requests they raise.

International procurement · later phase

Arriving in a later phase. The screens below are described here so the whole picture is in one place, but they are not yet part of Planet — you will not find them in the app today.

Head Office manages all imports, but your project's requirement is tracked at project level and appears on your dashboard.

  • PMR — Project Material Requirement. How a project asks for imported material. Raised by the project team, reviewed by HO project staff, sized by the Director — whose order quantity may be much larger than you asked for, because suppliers impose minimum orders. Your share is reserved to your project; the surplus becomes general company stock.
  • IPR — International Purchase Requisition. The overseas order. Becomes the purchase order once a signatory authorises it.
  • IRN — Import Receipt Note. The count against the packing list when a shipment clears customs in Malé.
  • SIN — Store Issue Note. How goods leave the Head Office store for a site.

Then you watch it move on your own dashboard: sized → ordered → advance paid → in production → shipped → arrived Malé → clearing → on manifest → received. Imported goods only become project cost when they land on your site — valued at their true landed cost, including freight, duty and clearing, not just the invoice price.

On your phone, and small things worth knowing

Planet is built for the site as much as the office. On a phone it stays legible and one-handed: today's obligations first, the camera one tap away for DPR photos, drafts held safely through a dropped signal.

  • Notifications tell you when something needs you — a document to approve, a boat coming, a shortage reported.
  • Everything is recorded. Who created, approved, changed, returned or voided anything, and exactly when. Nothing is deleted.
  • Conventions. Dates are dd/mm/yy. Times are 24-hour. Money is MVR unless a supplier is overseas.

If something goes wrong

Wrong figures on an issued document? Don't fight it. Revise it (MAR, MR), raise a new one linked to the old (IR), or ask your Administrator to void it with a reason. The register will show exactly what happened — which is the point. Questions go to your Administrator.
